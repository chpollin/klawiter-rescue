# Print pipeline: stages, measurement and reproduction of the print strand

The print strand turns the three PDF files described in [Print volumes](print-volumes.md) into lines, entry and apparatus records, structured objects, compiler claims and print-wiki claims. It is a separate strand of the repository, outside the production run. Since October 2026 it serves the wiki/print merge ([About](about.md), [Merged bibliography](print-wiki-merge.md)), and once the containment predicate for contents lines and the shared claim schema stand and the operator releases it, a frozen file of print addresses and evidenced claims without print text joins the production run; until then it is built and checked locally under `data/print/` (operator decision of 2026-10-24, [Production readiness](production-readiness.md)). Every output stays local under `data/print/derived/`, because lines, records and reports carry print text. The reports under `data/print/derived/reports/` are the source of every figure, and this document states results qualitatively or names the report and the date of the run.

## Stages

The analysis proceeds in stages, each with an inspectable output.

| Stage | Input and output | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| `p01 extract` | PDF to lines as JSONL, with volume, page, coordinates, font runs (each with the x of its first and last inked glyph, a new run after a gap wider than a column gutter), text and a defect flag, page hashes in its report | lossless deterministic base, running heads and page numbers marked rather than removed |
| `p02 view` (not built) | lines to Markdown per volume and section | reading view, derived and regenerable |
| `p03 records` | lines to entry records with stable identifiers, unaltered text and hash, heading context, and structured objects (see Structured objects) whose every value is cut from the entry with its span, producer and rule, inherited values kept apart; the indexes, front matter and tails to apparatus records | the records every later claim points back to |
| `dev_sample`, `probe_notations` | a frozen development sample stratified by volume and part, and counts of the notations per volume and part, both without the held-out pages and entries (`_heldout.py`) | the only material rules are written and inspected on |
| `bench_sample`, `bench_compare` | frozen page samples and blind annotations to scores and a disagreement list | measured agreement of boundaries, objects and references |
| `p04 wiki revisions` | the dump's main-namespace revisions to one line each, through the readers of the production extraction | the only place where verbatim print text survives in the wiki |
| `p05 errata` | the errata pages of Addendum I to correction claims against main-volume records, with the printed wrong and right readings verbatim | the compiler's own corrections |
| `p06 claims` | records to typed compiler reference claims (See, Rep:, Contents:, Correction to, Cont., work references) with resolved targets, all `proposed` | reference graph and a self-check of the segmentation |
| `p07 concordance` | records and revisions to print-wiki claims on two routes, alignment by distinctive word 5-grams over all revisions and title, year and a second field (imprint, translator, extent or page range) in the latest text or page title. A single-word title shorter than eight letters reads on into its subtitle and otherwise reaches only a page titled exactly that word. The second field is sought only in the wiki item of the match, the lines from the title to the year and, inside a verified publication slice, the slice's head with its imprint. It needs two agreeing tokens or one token so rare in the wiki lines that its chance presence in a five-line window stays under one percent, and a match without it stays a title-year block that keeps its token as evidence. The claim names the fields that agreed (place, publisher, translator, extent) and keeps their tokens apart. A collection header without address is not matched, because it stands for its volumes. A field match states the relation its wiki line supports: `contains` where the line names the print volume as the host of the item its page describes, `containedIn` where the line lists the print object as an item (a contents line of a verified publication, or a reprint line matched on the host title of a print contribution), and `samePublicationAs` otherwise. The object is the publication whose hash-verified slice holds the line, otherwise the page, and the identity candidates of one page are ranked by agreeing second-field tokens with the best marked as first candidates (a rank, no confirmation); `_viewer_data.py`, called by p12, projects these claims and the p08 conflicts for the viewer, keeping each claim's identifier, route, relation and state and adding no match of its own | concordance, with silver pairs from near-verbatim alignment measuring what the field route loses |
| `p07b overlap` | records, p07 claims and the published wiki layer to counts per volume and part of print objects by the strongest route a claim reaches them on (text, a field claim by its relation, a title-year block only, none), and of wiki publications and pages no claim names | what the concordance proposes as overlap, every count of proposed claims and none a confirmed identity; identifiers and counts only |
| `p08 field check` | silver pairs whose year and title agree, compared field by field (publisher, place, translator, extent) with the wiki publication of that year or the flat record | agreement of the two extractions per field and volume, not correctness against the book; it carries for Addendum II, and it found that edition lines under a main-volume work heading ("Bertelsmann: 383p. Gütersloh, 1952") yielded the imprint as title; p03 now reads publisher, place and year from such a line and takes the title from the work heading as an inherited value, skipping section, language, cross-reference and address-list headings |
| `p09 human sample` | concordance claims and records to two frozen blind samples, stratified by volume and by what the concordance proposed, and after both are answered a claim sheet | the blind sheets and the claim sheet of the human round, with the keys kept apart and a refusal to redraw |
| `p10 validate` | every object and apparatus item against its schema and the invariants over all records, every line of the files held by a record, known exceptions by identifier and reason, coverage per volume, type and field | the gate the structured objects pass; `test_print_objects.py` fails on any new violation, and `test_print_rules.py` pins each reading rule a review found wrong with an invented case |
| `p11 llm spans` | a frozen trial of objects without a type, the project's default LLM asked for offsets only, a versioned cache of identifiers, hashes and offsets | proposals for gaps the rules cannot read; no live call without the operator's consent |
| `p12 jsonld` | all objects as JSON-LD with one provenance node per value, validated on terms, RDF round trip and SHACL, and a local viewer | the export and the view of every value with its chain to passage, record and file |
| `review_sample`, `review_server`, `p13 review` | a frozen review sample of unseen entries, a localhost server that records checked review decisions from the viewer, and the measurement of field correctness on the complete sample objects | the human decision on whether a value is right in its context, and the only correctness figure of the fields |

`p01` exists as `pipeline/print_volumes/p01_extract.py` and runs in the optional uv group `print` (`python -m uv run --group print python pipeline/print_volumes/p01_extract.py`). Its lines and all reports go to `data/print/derived/`, which stays local with the PDFs. The section map with page ranges, hash prefixes and the compiler's contents ranges is `pipeline/print_volumes/sections.json`. `p01` uses `pdfplumber` under an MIT licence. PyMuPDF would be faster but is licensed under the AGPL, which does not fit an MIT repository. Docling is a candidate for the reading view only, because it drops the x-positions and partly the font runs that mark boundaries and can change its output with the version of its layout models. No reading view (`p02`) is built, and a comparison on the same pages would decide whether Docling enters it.

Segmentation stays lossless whatever decides the boundaries. Every line belongs to exactly one unit or is marked as a running head, heading or other non-entry, and the units reproduce the source text. A rule proposes each boundary, and number sequence, position, font, page breaks, headings and resolvable addresses check one another. Where they disagree, the case goes to a residue that review or an LLM decides, recorded as a separate layer in the manner of the `proposed`, `confirmed` and `contested` states of the reconciliation. Each file gets its own rule set, because the three files differ in layout. The tail of Addendum II and the indexes are parsed separately as apparatus records, never as entries; using the index references as a check of the entry boundaries is not built yet.

The language of an object comes from the language headings within its current work or section only. Form headings inside a language group (INDIVIDUAL STORIES, SELECTED WORKS), cross-references set as headings and address lists do not end the group. The rule was fixed on 2026-10-23, after the last language heading had also reached across a following work heading, so that an edition line under a German work heading read the language of the preceding translation group (main volume G 67 read Swedish), and after headings in capitals with a hyphen (SERBO-CROATIAN) had gone unrecognised. No existing language value changed; wrongly inherited ones became gaps and unrecognised ones became values.

After segmentation follow a concordance of print and wiki at entry level, first through the print addresses the wiki cites and then through title and year as candidates, and a report on what exists only in print, only in the wiki or in both, and where the versions differ. That report answers the question the strand starts from.

## Probes

Three deterministic probes in `pipeline/print_volumes/` test the files on the `p01` lines. They write their reports to `data/print/derived/reports/` and need no model call. The statements below describe the run of 2026-10-23, after an independent agent review of the same day had found several errors in the first run, among them running heads read as entries, numbering insertions counted as duplicates and claims the reports did not support. The corrected code and its reports are the source of the figures.

`p01_extract.py` writes every glyph of a page into exactly one line, and a second extraction is byte-identical. The first property holds by construction and guards the code rather than the data. Line grouping depends little on the vertical tolerance: between 2 and 3 pt the line count differs on a few pages of the main volume and Addendum I, while at 1 pt lines of the main volume split on about a third of its pages, presumably where substitute glyphs sit on a shifted baseline.

`probe_addresses.py` builds the address census. An entry start is a number, optionally preceded by its code (`L 464`), left of the part's hanging indent and on the longest chain of such numbers rising by at most ten per step. In the main volume it finds the entry starts of the earlier survey, with the gaps in G and L, and in Addendum I one start fewer than the survey. The apparent duplicates of its first run there are insertions with a letter suffix (`NA1 20a`). The highest number per section equals the transcribed contents of 1991 in every section except D and J, whose differences the files explain ([Print volumes](print-volumes.md), Entry boundaries). MA1 appears to run past its contents only because a line beginning "110 = G 336" joins the chain, and the probe now lists such jumps for review. In Addendum II only AA2, DA2 and the symposia list carry running numbers.

Almost every reference resolves against these starts, within the main volume, within Addendum I and from Addendum I into the main volume (rates in `reports/probe_addresses.json`). Almost every unresolved target lies above a section maximum or in a known gap, such as the reference to G 75, which the print replaces by a stub. Resolution therefore confirms the numbering and finds references into gaps, but it cannot detect a wrong boundary between two existing numbers. A content check, comparing the title cited before an address with the opening of the target entry, would test the segmentation itself and is not built yet.

`probe_roundtrip.py` gives every line one role and checks two invariants of the code: the roles rebuild the `p01` page texts, and each section's units reproduce its lines contiguously and in order. It then compares four single-signal boundary rules (number, hanging indent, vertical gap, bold start) per section, drops a rule that carries no signal there, and lets the vertical gap abstain at a page top, where no gap exists. The rules are not independent, since bold and number are only counted at the margin, so their agreement overstates certainty. The comparable figure across files is the number of disputed lines per unit (`reports/probe_roundtrip.json`).

| | Main volume | Addendum I | Addendum II |
|---|---|---|---|
| Carrying signals | number, indent, gap. Bold carries no signal from section I on | all four | gap and bold. No hanging indent, numbers only in AA2, DA2 and the symposia |
| Disputed lines per unit | low, concentrated in E, G, H and K | lowest | highest, GA2 the weakest |

`probe_signatures.py` profiles each unit by its citation forms: imprints, periodical citations, contributions that cite their host volume by address ("in Romans et Nouvelles [BA1 5]"), volumes that give only extent and year because the imprint stands in a collection header, and numbered sub-items outside a Contents list. The review checked a seeded sample of units and found that these patterns miscount often, for instance by taking the imprint of a reviewed book or of a letter holding as a publication, by reading "in" inside a title as a host reference, and by missing imprints with a bracketed place. The shares of units with several citations are therefore no measure of how many publications an entry describes. That an entry can describe several publications is established by direct reading of the files (collected editions with their printings, reprint lists in Addendum II, review entries in M). The bracketed sigla differ per volume: `[PF]` dominates the main volume, informant sigla dominate Addendum I, and in Addendum II brackets mostly hold supplied places such as `[Wien]`.

Four consequences follow for the parse stage. The address grammar is deterministic, and the numbering of the main volume and Addendum I is fully reconstructed, while boundaries between existing numbers still need a content check or the benchmark. Addendum II needs its own boundary rules based on gaps and bold, with a review residue that page tops dominate. An entry can describe several publications, so the record model separates entry and described object. Some entries depend on context outside themselves, a collection header or a host entry, and the parser has to carry that context.

## Benchmark

A held-out sample of pages (`bench_sample.py`, seeded) is annotated blind by two agents, one with the conventions of [Print volumes](print-volumes.md) and one with the PDFs only, and `bench_compare.py` scores boundaries, publication counts and references of the method against each annotator and the annotators against each other. The review judged the first sample too small and too little stratified: the largest sections fill the quota, so E of the main volume, NA1 and the numbered parts of Addendum II are missing, and the pages were part of the probe runs before they were held out. A second sample should draw entries or page pairs, oversample hard cases with recorded weights, keep a separate development set and be frozen before results are inspected. Agent annotation does not replace a human gold standard; disagreements and a control sample of agreements need a human decision.

The first run on 28 pages (2026-10-23, `reports/bench_compare.json`) gives three results. The two annotators agree on almost every unit boundary and on the publication class (none, one, several) of almost every unit; being two agents of the same kind, their agreement bounds rather than proves correctness. The method finds practically every boundary the annotators set, but sets additional ones: few in the main volume and Addendum I, about a quarter too many in Addendum II. The additional boundaries fall into two classes, numbered sub-items or volume lines on a line of their own ("[5]. CA2 …", "Vol. 2. …") and wrapped continuation lines after a line without closing punctuation ("1966), 307-312 [DAP].", "Review / of: …"). The publication count of probe_signatures.py agrees with the annotators' class for only about three units in five and is not usable as it stands. Annotator A recorded the title references of Addendum II ("FA2 Kaleidoskop 1936"), annotator B did not; the address grammar of the method covers neither. Any rule built from these error classes is tuned on the sample, so its effect has to be measured on a fresh sample.

The rules that followed came from those error classes and from an agent survey of development pages: sub-item openers never start a unit, unnumbered parts of Addendum II break only at vertical gaps, page tops are decided by how the previous page ends, and objects are read from numbered items by the scope of their label. A second sample of 44 pages (`bench/v2`, seeded, hard sections oversampled, disjoint from development pages) was frozen before these rules were scored and annotated blind by two agents. On it the annotators again agree on every boundary and on almost every publication class. An independent review of the milestone (2026-10-23) found three faults in the scorer, all corrected before the figures below: gold units with identical openings were all located on the first matching line, a unit's own printed address was counted as one of its references, and the method's objects and references were counted over the whole unit while the annotators saw only the sampled page. Five regular expressions of the rules also held a control character instead of a word boundary and so never matched; the correction left the scores on the sample unchanged. Measured after these corrections (`data/print/derived/reports/bench_compare_v2.json`), the method finds practically every boundary the annotators set. It sets very few extra ones in the main volume and Addendum I and somewhat under one in ten too many in Addendum II, most of them inside a single double-spaced paragraph that the gap rule reads as separate units; a gap rule that compares with the local line pitch would remove them. A date line ("5 September 1928.") once entered the number chain as an entry number. Reference recall and precision are near complete in all volumes. The publication class agrees with the annotators for roughly two to three units in four. A definition difference explains only part of that gap, since the annotators count later printings with data of their own and every address of an address-only reprint list. The rest are rule gaps: a host volume cited by address without year, unnumbered `REP:` and `EXC:` labels, double counting of "in [1].", further objects named only in prose, and unpublished holdings. Which of these count as objects is a policy question for the operator. `bench_compare.py` lists every disagreement for a human decision. Most are method against both annotators; a small part are splits between the annotators on the publication count. The sample is a single frozen draw annotated by agents, so the figures bound the method's agreement with careful readers on these pages; they are no measured correctness until a person has decided the disagreements.

## Structured objects

Since 2026-10-24 every object of the three volumes is a structured record of the schema `pipeline/print_volumes/schema/print-object.schema.json`, generated from the strict models in `_schema.py`. A fresh-context agent review read the schema before the readers were written; its findings on missing fields (reviewed work, event data, own language, address targets as lists, several periodicals and hosts, relations between objects of one entry) changed the schema, and its proposal to drop `inherited` as a producer was not adopted because the commission names four producers. Instead the producer `inherited` occurs only inside the object's inherited block, and an LLM proposes offsets into the object's own entry only.

Value
: The unit of every field, `{value, span, by, rule, from?, state?}`. The span is a character range of the record named in `from`, or of the object's own entry without it. A value without `from` lies inside the object's own span; `from` set to the own entry marks a value from the entry outside that span, such as the correspondent before a letter item or the label before an item. Text values equal their passage character for character, substitute-font defects and line-end hyphens included, and years equal the digits of theirs. Roles, types, kinds, languages and flags are derived from their passage by the rule they name.

Producer
: `rule` for a regular reading, `compiler` for the compiler's address notation read exactly, `inherited` for a value read on another record (collection header, work or language heading, the entry a later printing continues), `llm` for an offset an LLM proposed and a rule verified, always in the state `proposed`.

Object type
: A list from the closed vocabulary book edition, translation, contribution in a collection, periodical contribution, review, event, holding, reference and collection header, derived from the fields by one function that the validator reapplies. A translation or a review is also a book, a contribution or a periodical item, so an object can carry several types. A translation is read from a translator credit, or from a language heading only in the parts that list Zweig's works (A to J, N, O); secondary literature, reviews and letters in another language are no translations. An object whose citation form no rule recognises carries none.

Relation
: How an object relates to the one it follows in its entry, read from the governing label (reprint, excerpt, translation, edition, revised version, review, item), with the head object as target where the entry has one.

The readers in `_fields.py` follow the notations of each volume, which differ markedly ([Print volumes](print-volumes.md), Entry anatomy). The main volume cites periodicals by siglum and credits with `Tr.`, Addendum I spells periodicals out with their place in parentheses and credits with `Trl.`, and Addendum II spells credits out ("Edited with an afterword by") and cites reprints by section code, title, year and contents number. Each object's own citation runs up to the first label that introduces another publication or a cross-reference at the same bracket depth, so that the imprint of a reprint or of a reviewed book is never the object's. A label with a citation of its own (`FIE:`, `Rep:`, any `Sub. ed.` variant) splits an object, and a bracketed year (`[1925].`) is no item number. The split raised the agreement of the object counts with both blind annotators of the second benchmark in the main volume and Addendum I, most in Addendum I, and left Addendum II unchanged, with the entry boundaries unchanged (`reports/bench_compare_v2.json`). The rule answers error classes that the documented disagreements of that sample had named, so the second benchmark measures it as development; a held-out score needs a third sample frozen before it is scored. Cited titles reach the records on several paths (a host title, a title reference of Addendum II, an item read as its host, a periodical title); since the fourth review they pass one normalisation, which turns a leading section code into the address and a leading author into a person, so that a rule holds on every path, and one cut ends a cited title before a credit, edition or imprint sentence. The build is deterministic, and `p03_records.py --out` writes a trial build beside the records, so a refactoring is accepted by an identical build and a rule change by its value-level difference. Several findings of the development sample changed earlier stages at their root: cross-reference units that carry a year or a manuscript are entries, mixed-case one-line headings of the main volume (a language, a work title) scope the objects below them, a division of the letters ends every language group, and an edition line of the main volume inherits the title of the capitalised entry it continues.

Rules were written and inspected on the frozen development sample only (`dev_sample.py`), which, like every notation count and printed example, leaves out the pages of both benchmark samples and the entries of the blind sample (`_heldout.py`). The silver pairs of p07 and p08 served as measurement and, in aggregate, for finding defect classes, with held-out records excluded from both since 2026-10-24; each fix was then justified on the development sample.

`p10_validate.py` checks every object against the models and against invariants over all records: spans inside their source, text and years verbatim, roles, languages and kinds derivable from their passage, own values inside the object's span, inherited values only in the inherited block and from an existing record, languages only from a heading of the same part before the entry, types reproducible from the fields, every address target the record of that address, years within the bounds of the volume's state, and every rule a defined one. These checks establish consistency and provenance. Most of them reapply a table or function the readers use, so a value that is verbatim and in scope but taken from the wrong passage passes them, which a fresh-context review of 2026-10-24 showed on a random sample of non-held-out objects. Shape checks written apart from the readers therefore add a second layer. A title, the object's own or a host's, is no date, no siglum, no edition statement, opens with no section code and holds no imprint or page locator; an own title with no head read before it is no inverted personal name, and a host title is no list of names and opens with no "Surname, Forename." sentence. A periodical date holds a digit, except a month alone in a continued issue; pages hold a number; a place holds no digit; a name holds no digit; a publisher holds no credit. They found several classes of wrong values (repository sigla and sources as titles in the letters, notes read as periodical places, dates of letters read as years of printing, heads with roles or glosses in brackets read as titles), each fixed at its rule. Until the checks reached host titles, a wrong title that a rule moved into a host left their reach; extending them showed section codes, authors and page locators inside host titles and page values without a number, which one normalisation of host titles and the page patterns now remove. Known exceptions stand in `known_exceptions.json` by identifier and reason: substitute-font defects, values that genuinely have the shape, print errors, and named rule gaps left open. Coverage per volume, type and field is in `reports/p10_validate.json`; it is a reading rate, not correctness.

The measurements of 2026-10-24 compare the structured objects with the state before (`data/print/derived/baseline-2026-10-24/`), both without held-out records, and their figures stand in the reports under `data/print/derived/reports/`. The field comparison with the wiki on silver pairs (p08) rose for publisher, place, translator and extent in Addendum II, with more pairs comparable; in the main volume and Addendum I the pairs are too few to read a change. Recall of the concordance on silver pairs (p07) rose in the main volume, stayed within the step of one pair in Addendum I, whose silver pairs are few, and fell slightly in Addendum II, where the pair counts fell when the review sample was held out. In an earlier state of the rules most Addendum II matches lost against the old reading had rested on a year that is no year of publication (a year in a title, the date of writing, the year of an original or of a broadcast); the rest are real and open. On the second benchmark, now development, the object counts agree with the two blind annotators in most units (`reports/bench_compare_v2.json`). A second round of the same day, before the first review decision, closed reading gaps of collection headers, contents lists and heading context and changed the relations the concordance states (Known reading gaps). On the same wiki inputs as the code before it, the title-year recall on silver pairs stayed the same in every volume. The field recall moved by one pair per volume, down in the main volume and up in both addenda, the field comparison of p08 gained one comparable pair in Addendum II, and most field matches now state containment instead of identity, so the identity candidates fell to a fraction of the field claims (`reports/p07_concordance.json`, `reports/p07b_overlap.json`). The figures are agreement between readings of related text, not correctness, which only the human round measures.

The LLM stage (`_llm.py`, `p11_llm_spans.py`) is built and has not been run. It targets objects without a type, the only class where the rules demonstrably read nothing, sends the object's text to the project's default LLM and accepts nothing but offsets, each checked by rule against the passage (any text for titles and names, four digits of a plausible year for years, a credit word for roles). Accepted values fill only fields that neither the rules nor the inheritance filled, are merged after both, and keep the state `proposed`. The normal run applies the versioned cache `llm_cache.json` and makes no call; the cache holds identifiers, offsets and keys that are HMACs under a secret kept with the local print data, so that no short object text can be recovered from it by enumeration. A trial of 48 objects, 16 per volume, is drawn and frozen; it runs only with the operator's consent for that run. The environment variable that live mode requires guards against an accidental run; it cannot prove that consent.

`p12_jsonld.py` exports every object as JSON-LD (context `schema/print-context.jsonld`, shapes `schema/print-shapes.ttl`). Each value is a node with its literal, field, producer, plan (the rule or prompt as `prov:Plan`) and a W3C text position selector with the exact passage into its source record, which carries text, hash, PDF page and line and derives from its source file. The chain runs value, passage, record, file, and for inherited values through the record they were read from. Every chunk is validated on four levels: no undefined term, a JSON-LD to RDF round trip that keeps every object and value, the SHACL shapes, and a SPARQL constraint that every selector quotes its source exactly and every verbatim value equals its passage. Deliberately broken copies (a changed passage, a changed value, an LLM value without `proposed`, an empty span, a value of neither kind) fail. The local viewer and how `review_server.py` serves its code and data are described in [Print viewer](print-viewer.md). p12 writes only data into `data/print/derived/viewer/` and no copy of the code. `check_render.mjs` confirms that every value of every object gets a row, a complete chain and a readable label. Export and viewer stay under `data/print/derived/` because they carry print text.

The parts that hold no entries are records too (`_apparatus.py`, since 2026-10-24), so that every line of the three files is held by a record. Each index entry of the main volume and Addendum I is an apparatus record with one item: its term, the text before its first address, and its address groups, read by the address grammar of the entries, typed as index references and resolved to entry records. Front matter, the errata pages and the tails are kept as passages, paragraphs held verbatim and not read further; `p05_errata.py` reads the errata as claims. The indexes of a volume are read as one stream of columns, so an entry that runs onto the first page of the next index stays one; a page with a full-width heading is read in horizontal blocks, each block's left column before its right. Indexes that begin mid-page carry the top of their heading in `sections.json`. An entry opens at its column's margin where the line has the head of its index (an inverted name, a year, a language, a siglum or list number, in Addendum I a term before a colon after the blank line that separates entries there) or follows a closed entry without reading as a continuation; lines further in continue it, and the glyphs of a vertical strip join the entry open where the strip begins, until another entry opens. Page furniture is decided by its measured position per volume and part and a page number at one end, since the indexes of Addendum I begin close below their running heads. Addresses in index entries are read on a masked copy of the text, in which index labels ("See Index A5:"), the contents of item-locator brackets and the spaces between letter-spaced digits do not count, so that values stay verbatim while the shared grammar of the entries stays unchanged. Each apparatus record lists the pieces of lines it holds. `p10_validate.py` checks every apparatus item against its model (`schema/print-apparatus.schema.json`), its record's text and hash, verbatim values, index reference kinds on their address and address targets, that the pieces rebuild each record's text, and, with a furniture definition of its own, that every inked character of every apparatus line is held exactly once and furniture by no record, besides checking over all three files that every line outside the page furniture is held by an entry or an apparatus record. The shared grammar reads four-digit heads up to each section's range in the contents of 1991 (G runs to 1126), so the G addresses of the indexes resolve; a vertical strip that runs over a page joins its continuation without a space, which had broken numbers in a fifth of such strips; index headings are no longer read as entries with a term. Open: ranges wider than the grammar's limit, which in the Addendum I indexes are genuine printed blocks, and how a bracketed note after a number is held, an item locator ("[No. 3]", Addendum I) or a reference of its own ("[See D 21]", only in Index C of the main volume); both wait for the operator. `p12_jsonld.py` exports the items as `kp:ApparatusItem` with the same chain per value, and the viewer lists them beside the objects; review decisions cover the print objects only. A sixth fresh-context review checked a stratified sample of index records against the PDF and found errors in the column split, the strip attachment, the furniture, the part boundaries and above all the address reading of Addendum I; after the fixes, re-judged by agents on the same and on a fresh sample, the remaining errors are the open cases named above. Whether the attachment of strips matches the printed book cannot be checked without a printed copy.

A valid, span-bound value can still be the wrong value in its context, which no invariant shows and which a fourth fresh-context review of 2026-10-24 found in a sizeable share of a random sample (classes listed in the journal). Correctness is therefore decided by a person in the viewer (operator requirement of 2026-10-24). The decisions a reviewer takes in the viewer (confirm, correct by selecting the right passage, should not exist, open, and per object a missing value, a wrong boundary or complete) form an append-only log under `data/print/review/` beside the rule values, and [Print viewer](print-viewer.md) describes how the server checks and records them. `p12_jsonld.py` carries the decisions in force into the export as `prov:Activity` nodes with the reviewer as `prov:Agent`, checked by SHACL. A reviewed value carries one of the statement states confirmed or contested and the review action that produced it. A corrected value is confirmed, the confirmed value being the correction on the decision as a quoted passage, while the rule's reading stays in the chain as its predecessor. Correctness is measured only on a frozen review sample (`review_sample.py`), stratified by volume and part, one object per entry, drawn from entries that rule development had not seen (not the development sample, not any entry with a listed or open p10 violation, not the benchmark pages or the p09 blind sample) and held out from rule development from then on. Its supplements, the reviewers, adjudication and the figures `p13_review.py` reports follow [Merged bibliography](print-wiki-merge.md), Measurement. Reviewers see the rule values while deciding, so the figure measures acceptance after inspection; the blind sheets of p09 are the part that does not. Decisions on objects outside the frozen review sample are development feedback. `p13_review.py` counts them apart from the measurement, and rule work may learn from them, never from decisions on sample objects.

## Known reading gaps

These gaps were found by reading records against the PDF and the concordance claims. Each waits for a rule developed on the development sample and measured on the silver pairs.

Closed on 2026-10-24, before the first review decision, each change measured on the silver pairs against the earlier code on the same wiki inputs (Structured objects):

- A volume under a collection header that gives its imprints for periods, such as the first header of section A with one imprint up to 1950 and another from 1950, takes place and publisher from the one period that holds its year, marked inherited with the header as source. A year in two periods or in none takes nothing. A header now governs its volumes only up to the next heading other than a cross-reference, so the volumes of a later language group no longer inherit the imprint of an earlier group's header.
- The cross-references after a contents list, and those in a bracket of an address list ("(See …)"), are values of the object as well as compiler claims, marked as read from the entry outside the object's span. A "See" inside a bracket no longer ends a contents list, which had cut lists of Addendum II short at a bracketed original title. A titled item between the slashes of an address list is kept with its pages.
- A cross-reference set as heading no longer enters the heading context, so a run of them cannot push the section heading out of it.
- p07 seeks the second field only in the wiki item of the match, the title line, the year line and the lines between them, extended to the head of the verified publication slice they stand in. Before, a window of five lines let the place, publisher or translator of a neighbouring item agree.
- A field match to a contents or reprint line states containment (`contains` or `containedIn`) instead of identity with the page, and the claim names the fields that agreed.

Open:

- p07 reads a single-word title shorter than eight letters together with its subtitle since 2026-10-24. Most such titles carry no subtitle, and they still reach only a wiki page titled exactly that word.
- A contents line counts as one where it holds a contribution of a verified publication slice or names the print title as a host. On pages without a verified slice, a contents line that names its item first ("Title, pp. 11-110") cannot be told from the page's own description, so such a match still proposes the same publication as the page.
- Title-year blocks (`candidateFor`) state no relation. They stay local and weak, and the human round decides what they mean.
- An address contents list of a multi-volume set runs over its volume statements, so the titled items and addresses of later volumes stand among the contents of the set, and a titled item that shares a slash group with an address of the next volume is dropped.
- The strata of the first p09 blind sample were drawn on the predicates of 2026-10-23, and their sizes were not kept. `--strata` reconstructs them from the oldest claims kept, and in Addendum II the reconstruction agrees poorly with the recorded strata, so the blind weights are approximate. The measurement compares answers with the current proposals and treats the drawing strata as weights. A supplement drawn on the current claims, with a stratum for entries without a read year, brings the title-year route to forty claims per volume; the field route falls short in every volume and is not admissible for automatic confirmation. Containment claims form no measured route, because a blind answer names the page of the same publication.
- References from Addendum II into the main volume resolve markedly less often than those inside one volume, and their residue still has to be sorted into gaps of the print and parser errors (`reports/probe_addresses.json`).

## Reproducing the strand

Anyone with the three PDFs can rebuild the strand on another machine. The files are taken from the repository `chpollin/SZD`, folder `klawiter/`, commit `58234e73`, and placed in `data/print/` under the names `sections.json` gives ([Print volumes](print-volumes.md), The three files). `p01_extract.py` refuses a file whose SHA-256 prefix differs. The stages then run in order: `p01_extract`, `p03_records`, `p04_wiki_revisions` (needs the wiki pipeline's stage 01), `p05_errata`, `p06_claims`, `p07_concordance`, `p07b_overlap`, `p08_field_check`, `p10_validate` and `p12_jsonld`. `p11_llm_spans.py` runs only in its frozen mode without the operator's consent. `p14_gate.py --export` then shows that the rebuild matches: the same inputs, two byte-identical rebuilds of records and export, a green validation with its ledger, the frozen sample identifiers against their pins, and no print text in a versioned file. The frozen samples themselves (`data/print/derived/samples/`) are no output of a stage and must be copied with the data, since redrawing them would end their role as held-out material. Without the PDFs nothing of the strand can be rebuilt or checked, and that remains its limit as long as the rights keep the files out of the repository.

## Decisions outside this strand

Admitting the print as a second documented source changes the data integrity rule of the repository, which so far admits bibliographic values only from the MediaWiki source. That decision belongs to the operator and is recorded in [Production readiness](production-readiness.md) once taken. Publishing structured data derived from the volumes raises the same rights question as publishing the PDFs, since the compiler's annotations and the compilation as a whole may be protected even where single bibliographic facts are not. The model that relates print and wiki without merging them, and the plan toward a merged Klawiter bibliography in Stefan Zweig Digital, are recorded in [Merged bibliography](print-wiki-merge.md).
